# Cover Brief v1 — The Wish Fairy and Dewy Dear

**Status:** proposal only — not approved artwork

## Objective

Create a WNPH paperback cover that feels like a real published object and is visually rooted in the character of the recovered 1922 book without pretending that the historical binding or artwork is the physical specification of the new edition.

The result should support both print production and the Edition Presentation System. The design must be capable of becoming a continuous paperback wrap once final trim, page count, paper, and spine width are known.

## Source character worth preserving

Historical reference evidence describes the 1922 Wish Fairy Series as small-format children's books with grey cloth, green lettering, a multicolored book-specific image/appliqué, and a matching white coated dust jacket. The surviving source also contains a color frontispiece and six additional governed color-plate placements.

These are design references, not instructions to reproduce a historical cover exactly.

## WNPH design direction

The first proposal should test a **recovered storybook** direction:

- use one governed public-domain source illustration or a carefully derived visual motif as the central pictorial element;
- preserve generous paper-colored space rather than filling the cover with a synthetic solid-color field;
- use period-aware but highly readable typography;
- retain a visible relationship between image, title, and physical paper stock;
- avoid glossy/plastic CGI treatment;
- let the final mockup show the thin cover stock, real page block, slight spine crease, and paper edges;
- build the spine as part of the same design system, not as an arbitrary color bar.

## Physical realism acceptance test

A successful mockup must still look credible when the artwork is visually ignored. The viewer should be able to see that it is a manufactured paperback because:

- the cover is a thin separate wrapper;
- the page block is independently visible;
- the fore-edge/top/bottom pages have paper texture;
- the spine has appropriate thickness and a subtle binding crease;
- light and shadow interact with the object rather than being baked into a fake rectangular graphic;
- the artwork appears printed on the cover stock.

## Decisions intentionally deferred

- trim size;
- final page count;
- spine width;
- paper stock;
- cover stock;
- matte/gloss/uncoated finish;
- final illustration selection;
- typography family;
- final palette;
- back-cover copy and marks.

Those decisions should be made as publication choices and then promoted into the EPP; they are not inferred here.
